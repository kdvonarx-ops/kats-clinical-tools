    About This Tool

    The CVD Risk & Lipid Management tool combines two evidence-based clinical decision aids — the AHA PREVENT risk calculator and the 2026 ACC/AHA Statin Chooser — into a single offline, Epic-ready resource for primary and secondary cardiovascular prevention.

    Jump to: The VIBES board · Epic integration

    Part 1 — AHA PREVENT Risk Calculator

    What PREVENT Is

    The AHA Predicting Risk of CVD EVENTs (PREVENT) equations are a validated, race-free tool for estimating 10-year and 30-year cardiovascular risk in US adults aged 30–79 without known CVD. Developed from 3.28 million participants across 25 diverse US datasets (1992–2017), PREVENT replaces the 2013 Pooled Cohort Equations (PCEs) as the preferred risk tool per the 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline.

    Total CVD

    Atherosclerotic CVD plus new-onset heart failure combined. Best overall cardiovascular burden estimate.

    ASCVD

    Coronary heart disease and stroke from arterial plaque. Primary metric for statin therapy decisions.

    Heart Failure

    New-onset HF risk. Predicted separately for the first time in PREVENT — driven by HTN, diabetes, obesity, and CKD.

    Statin initiation may be considered at ≥3% 10-yr ASCVD per 2026 ACC/AHA. The 2026 guideline notably shifts the borderline threshold from 5% (2018) down to 3%, reflecting improved calibration of PREVENT versus the PCEs.

    Equation Implementation & Validation

    • Uses the simplified logistic regression approximation published in the Khan et al. 2024 supplemental materials — not the full Cox competing-risks model, but R² >0.99 against it
    • 10-year equations: Supplemental Table S24, verified against worked example in Table S25
    • 30-year equations: Tables S12F–J (base, UACR, HbA1c, SDI, Full variants)
    • Formula: Risk = exp(log-odds) / (1 + exp(log-odds)) — logistic function applied to the linear predictor
    • Cholesterol converted internally from mg/dL to mmol/L (×0.02586); eGFR entered directly
    • Verified against the AHA online calculator for all three supplemental reference cases — results match to within 0.1 percentage point across all 18 risk estimates
    • Runs entirely client-side — no patient data transmitted

    PREVENT Limitations

    • Primary prevention only — contraindicated in known CVD, LVEF <40%, CAC ≥300, pathogenic genetic variant, ESKD, or life expectancy <1 year
    • May underestimate risk in American Indians, some South Asians, and some Puerto Ricans
    • SDI uses an Austin-area (Travis County) ZIP lookup table; ZIP codes outside it are not scored, and values may not match current national SDI

    Part 2 — Statin Chooser

    What the Statin Chooser Does

    The Statin Chooser translates the 2026 ACC/AHA/Multisociety Dyslipidemia Guideline into a structured treatment recommendation. It covers five clinical pathways, determines statin intensity and LDL-C target, and generates a stepwise escalation plan including ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitor thresholds — all with safety flags for statin intolerance, pregnancy, liver disease, CKD, and drug interactions.

    Part 3 — Lp(a) & ApoB Screening

    Lipoprotein(a) — Lp(a)

    Lp(a) is an independent, genetically-determined cardiovascular risk factor not captured by standard lipid panels. The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline recommends universal one-time Lp(a) measurement for all adults — it does not need to be repeated as levels are stable over a lifetime.

    • Elevated threshold: ≥125 nmol/L or ≥50 mg/dL
    • Prevalence: ~20% of the general population has elevated Lp(a)
    • Clinical impact: Elevated Lp(a) is a risk enhancer that supports more aggressive LDL-C lowering. In secondary prevention patients not at LDL-C goal on max statin + ezetimibe, add a PCSK9 inhibitor — which also reduces Lp(a) by ~25%
    • Lp(a)-specific therapies: No FDA-approved Lp(a)-specific agents currently available in the US; RNA-targeted therapies (olpasiran, pelacarsen) in late-stage trials

    Apolipoprotein B — ApoB

    ApoB reflects total atherogenic particle number and can be more informative than LDL-C in certain clinical scenarios — particularly when LDL-C may underestimate true risk. Because of cost, ApoB is not a routine order for primary care given cost; it should be ordered in consultation with cardiology when clinically indicated.

    Clinically indicated when:

    • Triglycerides ≥150 mg/dL — LDL-C underestimates atherogenic particle burden
    • Diabetes or metabolic syndrome — increased small dense LDL particles
    • Borderline/intermediate primary prevention risk — helps clarify statin decision when LDL-C alone is uncertain
    • Low LDL-C (<70 mg/dL) in a high-risk patient — confirms adequate particle reduction on therapy
    • FH or severe hypercholesterolemia — confirms therapy response

    Targets (2026 ACC/AHA): <80 mg/dL in high-risk patients; <65 mg/dL in very high-risk patients.

    Part 4 — The VIBES Board

    How the board works

    VIBES (Validated Indices for Benchmarking Evidence & Scoring) are the score tiles on the board. Patient data is entered once in the Vitals Hub; each tile reads what it needs, shows its result when complete, and flags missing or out-of-range values. Checked tiles go into the VIBE Score Card, a printable summary built by the Vibe Check.

    Using CHA₂DS₂-VASc and HAS-BLED

    • CHA₂DS₂-VASc applies to AF or atrial flutter. It is not used for patients with a mechanical heart valve or moderate–severe mitral stenosis, who need warfarin regardless of score.
    • The 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS guideline recommends oral anticoagulation at an annual stroke risk ≥2% (score ≥2 in men, ≥3 in women) and considers it reasonable at 1–2% (score 1 in men, 2 in women). Female sex is treated as a risk modifier rather than a risk factor on its own.
    • The 2024 ESC guideline uses CHA₂DS₂-VA, which drops the sex point. This tool follows the US guideline and notes the difference.
    • A HAS-BLED score ≥3 marks high bleeding risk. It is a prompt to correct modifiable risks (uncontrolled blood pressure, labile INR, antiplatelet or NSAID use, alcohol) and to follow more closely, not a reason by itself to withhold anticoagulation.

    Score limitations

    • HFpEF-ABA sets no cutoffs in its derivation paper; the low/intermediate/high bands shown (<25%, 25–80%, >80%) are as cited from the 2026 ACC HFpEF pathway. It is a screening estimate and does not replace echocardiography or natriuretic peptides.
    • H₂FPEF needs echocardiographic E/e′ and PASP and applies only when LVEF is ≥50%; it was derived in a referral population, so probabilities may run higher than in primary care.
    • Each score was derived in a specific population; performance may differ in younger patients, other health systems, or populations underrepresented in derivation cohorts.
    • The perioperative scores predict different outcomes (RCRI: major cardiac complications; MICA: MI or cardiac arrest; AUB-HAS2: death, MI, or stroke) and should not be read as the same measurement.
    • CHA₂DS₂-VASc and HAS-BLED share several risk factors, so patients at high stroke risk are often at higher bleeding risk too; the net benefit of anticoagulation usually still favors treatment.

    General Limitations & Disclosures

    Limitations

    • Clinical decision support only — does not replace clinical judgment or patient-clinician shared decision-making
    • PREVENT is validated for primary prevention in adults aged 30–79 without known CVD, severe subclinical CVD, ESKD, or limited life expectancy
    • PREVENT may underestimate risk in American Indians, some South Asians, and some Puerto Ricans
    • Statin Chooser logic reflects published guideline text; individual patient factors (drug interactions, comorbidities, patient preference) must always be considered
    • SDI lookup uses an Austin-area (Travis County) ZIP table; ZIP codes outside it are not scored
    • This tool is not a substitute for a formal lipid clinic or cardiology consultation in complex cases

    Phase 2 — SmartText Auto-Population (Epic build, moderate IT lift)

    Epic's SmartText can embed SmartLinks that pull live chart data — labs, vitals, medications, problem list — directly into the note when the clinician opens it. This means when a clinician uses the .CVDLIPID note template, the patient's most recent LDL-C, SBP, BMI, HbA1c, diabetes status, and current statin are pre-filled automatically. The clinician still runs the calculator to get the risk number, but all the inputs are already there — no chart-hunting required.

    Important distinction: SmartLinks pull and display data — they cannot run calculations. The PREVENT equations and statin logic still require the clinician to open the calculator tool. Phase 3 (SMART on FHIR) is needed to auto-calculate risk. Phase 2 eliminates manual data transcription — Phase 3 eliminates the calculation step entirely.

    Note
    Disclaimer

    This tool is clinical decision support for noncardiac surgery. It does not replace clinical judgment, institutional protocols, or consultation with surgery, anesthesia, and pharmacy. Estimates come from published models and are only as accurate as the data entered. Antiplatelet management in patients with coronary stents should be a multidisciplinary, shared decision.

    GLP-1 receptor agonists follow the 2024 multisociety guidance: most patients continue them, and those at elevated risk of delayed gastric emptying get a 24-hour liquid diet and an anesthesia alert. Neuraxial and deep-block DOAC holds follow the 2025 ASRA 5th edition, which distinguishes therapeutic from prophylactic doses. Enoxaparin doses are rounded to the nearest 10 mg — confirm against your pharmacy's syringe sizes. When GI endoscopy is checked, low-dose aspirin is continued (ACG–CAG 2022). Other hold schedules are written for surgery; for endoscopy, follow ACG–CAG guidance (P2Y12 inhibitor held on DAPT, DOACs briefly interrupted) and defer to the endoscopist.
